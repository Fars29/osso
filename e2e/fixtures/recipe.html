One-Pot Lemon Chicken Orzo

Updated 14 September 2026 · 35 minutes · Serves 4

Every summer, my grandmother would open the windows of her kitchen in Liguria and the whole street would know what was for dinner. I still remember the smell of lemons on her hands, and the way she would hum something from the radio while the pan hissed. She never wrote anything down, which is a problem when you are trying to recreate a dish twenty years later from memory alone.

This recipe has been on my list for years, and honestly I don't know why it took me so long to share it with you. It has become a weeknight staple in our house, and my kids ask for it at least twice a week, which for a nine-year-old and a six-year-old counts as a standing ovation. There is something magical about a dish that comes together in one pot, with one wooden spoon, in the time it takes to set the table and argue about homework.

A quick word on what makes this version different from the hundred others online. Most one-pot orzo recipes boil the pasta in plain water and add the chicken at the end, which gives you a perfectly fine but slightly flat result. Here the orzo is toasted in the chicken fat first, then cooked in stock, so every grain carries the flavour of the sear. The lemon goes in off the heat, which is the single detail my grandmother was strict about, and which I have since confirmed the hard way.

Ingredients

  • 500 g boneless, skin-on chicken thighs, patted dry
  • 300 g dried orzo
  • 750 ml chicken stock, warm
  • 1 large unwaxed lemon, zested and juiced (about 3 tablespoons of juice)
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
  • 40 g Parmigiano Reggiano, finely grated, plus more to serve
  • A handful of flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon fine sea salt, plus more for the chicken
  • Freshly ground black pepper

Method

  1. Season the chicken thighs generously with salt on both sides and let them sit at room temperature while you chop the onion and garlic.
  2. Heat the olive oil in a wide, heavy pan over medium-high heat until it shimmers.
  3. Sear the chicken skin-side down for 6 minutes without moving it, or it will not brown; then flip and cook for 2 more minutes and transfer to a plate.
  4. Lower the heat to medium, add the onion to the fat in the pan and cook for 4 minutes until soft, then add the garlic for 30 seconds.
  5. Add the orzo and stir for one minute so it toasts slightly before the stock goes in.
  6. Pour in the warm stock, add the teaspoon of salt, scrape the bottom of the pan and bring to a simmer.
  7. Return the chicken to the pan skin-side up and simmer uncovered for 12 to 14 minutes, stirring every few minutes so the orzo does not stick to the bottom.
  8. Turn off the heat, then stir in the lemon zest, the lemon juice and the Parmigiano; do not add the lemon juice while the pan is still on the heat, or the sauce will turn bitter.
  9. Rest for 3 minutes, scatter with parsley and black pepper, and serve straight from the pan.

Tips and substitutions

The orzo should be loose and creamy when it comes off the heat, almost like a risotto; if it looks dry, add a splash of stock or water before the cheese goes in. Chicken breast works but dries out easily, so cut the simmer to 9 minutes and slice it before adding it back. For a vegetarian version, swap the chicken for 400 g of halved cherry tomatoes added in the last five minutes and use vegetable stock. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to three days; add a splash of stock when reheating, because the orzo will have absorbed all the liquid overnight. I do not recommend freezing this one, the pasta turns mushy.

People always ask about the pan. Anything wide with a heavy base will do; a 28 cm sauté pan or a shallow casserole is ideal, a deep narrow pot is not, because the orzo will cook unevenly. If your stock is unsalted, you may need up to 2 teaspoons of salt in total, so taste before serving.
