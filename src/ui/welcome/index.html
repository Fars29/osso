osso

Just the bone.

Most of what you read online is filler. Osso strikes it out, on every page, and leaves what you came for. Two choices, and you are done.

  1. Your TypeSafe key

    Osso asks a model, Jev, about every sentence of a page. It runs on a key of your own: no account with us, no server of ours. Get a key at typesafe.ai

    Saving a key turns Osso on. When it reads a page, what leaves this machine is the page's title, its language and the words the page displays, sent to api.typesafe.ai only, over your key. Never the page's address, and never what you type into it. The key stays in this browser.

  2. When should Osso read a page?

    • Never sent: the page's address, and anything you type into a page. Osso reads the words a page displays, never the values in its fields.
    • Not read: a page showing a password, card or one-time-code field.
    • Held back: pages that look private: an account area, an intranet, a page its site hides from search engines. Osso asks before reading those, and goes by how a page is built, never by what it talks about: an article about banks is an article.
    • Off on a list of well-known mail, document, chat, code, search, video and social sites. A site not on it is read like any other. Edit the list

    Those checks catch the shapes private pages usually have, not every one. A site built differently can slip through, and then that page's text is sent like any other. Where the text itself is private, choose only when I click, or switch Osso off for that site.
